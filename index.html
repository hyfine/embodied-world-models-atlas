EMBODIED AI × WORLD MODELS　/　INTERACTIVE FIELD GUIDE

From world models
to world action models

To slide a cup into a circle, a robot needs a push that can produce the desired change. A world action model (WAM) couples future prediction to action generation: some systems pass a predicted goal to a policy; others learn futures and actions jointly.

WM-6 / Coupling robot futures to actions

DreamZero

At inference, condition the next chunk on history and new observations; actions go to execution and predicted video serves modeling or inspection. This is not explicit MPC over competing videos. Count generation, communication and actuation latency separately.

EAI-1 / Language and grounded planning

ReKep

After motion, update keypoints and solve again; no fresh VLM query is required at each control step. The public simulator supplies tracking and collision geometry that physical robots must estimate.

See the evidence and paper →
EAI-2 / Vision-language-action policies

OpenVLA

Demonstrations train the policy offline. The Bridge loop refreshes observations between actions; the base model takes a single image per query, does not predict future images and does not update weights online.

See the evidence and paper →
EAI-3 / Visuomotor policies & demonstration interfaces

Diffusion Policy

Iterate scheduler.step from a noisy trajectory. predict_action unnormalizes the sampled sequence and returns a slice beginning at To−1 of length n_action_steps for execution before refreshing observations. Predicted and open-loop execution horizons are different.

See the evidence and paper →
EAI-4 / Trajectory data, cross-robot pretraining & adaptation

Octo

Re-query on a rolling observation window; denoising iterations are not sensor feedback. New sensor/action heads are adapted offline.

See the evidence and paper →
EAI-5 / Legged & humanoid control

Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA)

The fast loop reads new state without waiting for the slow thread. No true friction input or online weight updates.

See the evidence and paper →
EAI-6 / Navigation & mobile manipulation

OK-Robot

Grasp and drop use fresh local perception, not a continuously updated semantic map or a general failure-retry loop.

See the evidence and paper →
EAI-7 / Simulators & evaluation

CALVIN (long-horizon language control)

The next action uses fresh observations. Only success advances the instruction; timeout truncates the chain. No policy updates in evaluation.

See the evidence and paper →
EAI-8 / RL post-training

SERL (real-world online training)

Physical observations close the control loop; the asynchronous learner sends updated weights. No imagined transition or mandatory per-step human correction.
